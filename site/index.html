There was never a golden age of photographic truth. Retouching is as old as the negative; Conan Doyle was taken in by two schoolgirls with paper cutouts in 1917.

What photographs had was never self-evidence. It was friction — faking one took a darkroom, a skill and an afternoon — plus a scaffolding of picture desks, wire services and libel law that made lying expensive by other means.

Generative models did not make images forgeable. They made forgery free, and fast. The friction is gone, the scaffolding is thinner, and what remains is a medium that was always susceptible, now cheap to abuse at volume.

So the question isn't how to restore a credibility photographs never quite had. It's what you put in its place. Detection is the obvious candidate and the wrong one: a detector is a classifier guessing at the output of a generator, and it gets worse exactly as the generator gets better.

Provenance is the other answer — not proof that a scene was real, but a checkable record of where a file came from. A smaller claim than most people want, and the only one that survives contact with someone determined to lie.

How it works

The shutter path

Six things happen on the device before the file exists. Sealing and verifying both work with the radio off — no accounts, no analytics, no launch-time network calls.

1

Hash the exact bytes

SHA-256, chunked — checking a video never loads the whole file into memory.

src/lib/fileHash.ts
2

Record what the sensors said

Time, GPS (opt-in), compass heading, barometric altitude, a motion signal.

src/sensors/
3

Sign it in the Secure Enclave

ECDSA P-256. The private key is generated on the chip and can't be extracted — signing happens there, not in app memory.

src/lib/deviceKey.ts · modules/secure-enclave/
4

Write Content Credentials into the file

A real C2PA manifest — CBOR claim, hard binding, COSE_Sign1 — inside the JPEG, PNG, MP4, MOV or M4A itself. Any C2PA reader can open it.

src/c2pa/
5

Add a second signature for later

ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) over the same commitment, so a future break of P-256 doesn't quietly invalidate an archive.

src/lib/pq.ts
6

Anchor the time

An RFC 3161 countersignature when there's a network; an OpenTimestamps receipt for independent proof-of-existence. Offline, it signs without and says so.

src/lib/timestamp.ts · src/lib/ots.ts

The interface

The design is open too

Getting the cryptography right is half the problem. The other half is telling someone what a signature actually means without overstating it — and that lives in the interface. All of it is in the repo: tokens, primitives, screens.

Five rungs, never a badge

A checkmark is an institutional gesture. It works by borrowing the authority of some body that has supposedly done the checking — a notary, a ratings board, a verification team. No such body exists here, so a badge would be a piece of borrowed furniture, and the borrowing would be the dishonest part, not the cryptography.

The verdict surface is a ladder instead: five separate questions, each with its own answer, and an unreached rung that says why. The card carries its title and limits inside the frame, where they survive being screenshotted.

Words the product may never use

A test fails the build if verified, authentic, trusted, proven, real, secure or guaranteed appears in a verdict position. Operations that genuinely ran keep their precise verbs — "signature mathematically valid" is allowed, because it happened. It sounds fussy; it is the one thing that kept the copy honest as the feature count grew, because the pressure to round a qualified result up to a confident one is constant.

◍ intact ◍ unproven ◍ not checked ◍ unsigned

Unsigned is neutral grey — never red. Absence of a credential is not evidence of tampering.

What you can take

The parts, and what each is good for

Most of this is platform-neutral TypeScript with no build step. If you're writing a signer, a verifier, or an Android port, these are the pieces worth stealing.

PathWhat it is, and why you might want it
src/c2pa/A complete, dependency-light C2PA implementation: CBOR claims, COSE_Sign1, JPEG APP11/JUMBF, PNG caBX, BMFF/MP4 embedding with chunk-offset repair, and the verifier. Independently checked against c2patool on every CI run.
src/lib/The crypto plumbing, all pure TS: a strict X.509 chain verifier, an RFC 3161 token verifier, COSE/DER, ECDSA signing, an ML-DSA-65 post-quantum layer, AES-256-GCM, canonical JSON. No WebCrypto, no network.
src/provenance/The pipeline that turns a capture into a sealed record — orchestration, schema, background seal queue, detached manifests, and a differential oracle that cross-checks two independent engines against each other.
src/vault/Encrypted on-device storage. Media, records and thumbnails are all sealed; plaintext only ever exists in a cache folder shredded on lock.
src/theme.ts · src/components/ · app/The whole interface: design tokens with a dual light/dark palette, the UI kit, and the screens — capture, inspect, exhibits, settings, onboarding.
modules/The native Swift: Secure Enclave keygen and signing, App Attest, the AVFoundation capture engine, the raw-audio sink, and the C2PA Rust binding.
server/A zero-dependency App Attest relay in one file. Runs anywhere Node 20 runs; you can host your own or skip it entirely.
tests/The lab: 27 suites that run the real shipping code against real forgeries, with only device services shimmed.

Build on it

Worth building next

This is most useful as parts. If any of these appeal, take them — no coordination needed, no permission to ask.

An Android port

The provenance core is platform-neutral TypeScript. What's needed is the equivalent of modules/secure-enclave against Android Keystore and Play Integrity — the record format and verifier carry over unchanged.

A standalone verifier

Wrap src/c2pa/verifyAsset.ts in a CLI so anyone can check a file with npx, no app required. The verifier is already free of platform dependencies.

Better time anchoring

TSA root anchoring is the honest gap — there's no mature public root store for timestamping the way there is for the web PKI. A curated, auditable trust list would help every project in this space, not just this one.

Newsroom key custody

Rosters and revocation are implemented and lab-pinned, but the operational question — how a desk actually enrolls and retires a photographer's key — deserves real field design.

Take the interface

The ladder, the banned verdict words, the unreached-rung disclosures — the part that took longest to get right. Lift it; none of it is specific to this app.

Attack it

Make a forged file verify and I'd genuinely like to see it. The suites in tests/ show the shape of a good repro.

Run it yourself

Five minutes, offline

The lab stages the real shipping code and rewires only device services — keychain, filesystem, device model — to small shims. Every cryptographic operation is the code that runs on the phone.

It signs fresh media with a random key on each run, then attacks it: flipped bits, transplanted manifests, self-issued "O=Reuters" certificates, truncated files, hostile parsers.

# node 20+, ffmpeg
node tests/stage.mjs
cd tests/.staged && npm install

./node_modules/.bin/tsx test-verification.mts
→ 146 passed, 0 failed

Add c2patool to the path and the independent-verifier checks run too, cross-checking our output against the reference C2PA implementation.

The remaining problem

Two different attacks, and only one of them is closed

A signature proves custody of bytes. It cannot prove what the lens was pointed at — and that isn't a caveat to bolt on the end, it's the shape of the work that's left.

largely closed

Injection — fake photons

Frames substituted below the API, virtual camera drivers, replaced sensor modules. Enclave signing and hardware attestation are aimed squarely at this: they establish that a genuine device and a genuine build produced the bytes.

open

Rephotography — real photons, fake scene

A genuine sensor faithfully recording a monitor. Every cryptographic guarantee here holds perfectly, and the result is a valid seal over a real recording of something that never happened.

Closing injection doesn't reduce rephotography. It makes it the only remaining path, which concentrates attacker effort there. Attestation makes the geometry work more necessary, not less.

And rephotography is geometric, which is the encouraging part. A screen three metres away is a plane. Two lenses with a known baseline can measure planarity directly — a homography residual is a different order of evidence from a monocular depth estimate or a moiré heuristic, and it degrades honestly: past a few metres the answer is insufficient, never passed.

That's what the stereo capture path and the committed second view are for. The verifier keeps custody and scene rigorously separate, because collapsing them into one number is how this whole category loses its credibility.

Where this build actually stands

Two checks aren't performed: TSA root anchoring and certificate revocation. Both are named on every verification rather than skipped quietly.

Sensors are claims. Time, GPS, heading and altitude are what the device reported, bound into the signature. The binding is real; whether the device told the truth is a separate question.

Stereo capture is unvalidated on iPhone 17 / iOS 26. The verification side is lab-tested; the capture side moved to Apple's virtual dual-wide device graph and hasn't been confirmed in the field.

Beta software, written with AI assistance and held to account by the test lab, an independent reference verifier, and a differential oracle. No conformance certification.
