Camera pipeline · iPhone 17 / iOS 26 · round 3

The Dead Secondary Stream

Your last two rounds eliminated wiring and formats correctly. There is one variable neither round controlled for — and the field log shows it was set against you the whole time.

The finding. Your 0.18.1 failure dump ends with flag.photoMaxDimensionsPolicy=false. That flag gates the ≤12 MP maxPhotoDimensions clamp — the mitigation you wrote in 0.15.2 for the mechanism you yourself named "the structural suspect for BOTH field failures": an unclamped 48 MP photo-stream reservation starving everything else on a live multi-cam graph.

The flag's getter defaults to true and does so correctly (it checks object(forKey:) != nil before reading, so an unset key returns true). A false reading therefore means the key was explicitly written false on that device — almost certainly a leftover from a wave-7 A/B isolation. It lives in the exhibit.debug UserDefaults suite, which survives every TestFlight update. Only deleting the app clears it.

So the most likely story is not that your fixes failed. It is that rounds 1 and 2 were both tested on a device carrying a persisted flag that disabled the one mitigation aimed at this exact failure — while iPhone 17 is the first generation whose ultra-wide is a 48 MP sensor, making the unclamped reservation far larger than on any hardware where this code previously worked.

02 — LiveWhat is left, ranked

Everything below survives the eliminations above. Ordered by probability given your evidence.

H1Photo-output resource reservation starves the secondary video streammost likely

Your graph carries six consumers on one multi-cam session: two AVCaptureVideoDataOutputs, two AVCapturePhotoOutputs, a PiP preview connection, and audio. WWDC19-249 is explicit that photo outputs are among the most expensive things you can attach, because the session reserves ISP and bandwidth for the configured photo stream whether or not you are capturing.

On iPhone 17 the ultra-wide moved from 12 MP to 48 MP. With the clamp disabled, the secondary photo output reserves against a 48 MP format on the same physical camera whose video stream then never delivers. That is a coherent mechanism for absent-not-dropped, deterministic from frame one, and preview-alive: preview-layer connections are the cheap, forgiving path and survive pressure that kills a data output.

To your credit the clamp is applied to both photo outputs when enabled (applyFullResPhotoPolicy is called for primary and secondary alike). The problem is only that it was switched off.

TestDelete the app entirely (not an update — deletion is what clears the exhibit.debug suite), reinstall, confirm the Diagnostics screen shows photoMaxDimensionsPolicy=true, and re-run. This is a ten-minute test and it is the first thing to do.
H2Total graph cost — and you only gate on half of itlikely

You throw when session.hardwareCost > 1.0, but systemPressureCost is logged and never gated. Both matter: a graph can sit under the hardware budget while system pressure forces the session to shed a stream. Shedding is silent — no exception, no drop callbacks — which is exactly your signature.

The fellUp path compounds this. If no multi-cam format on the 48 MP ultra-wide fits ≤1280×720, you fall up to the smallest multi-cam format on the device, which on this hardware could be substantially larger than the 720p budget you intended. Your 0.18.3 census logs this, so the answer is probably already sitting in a field log.

TestRead the multiCam census: line from a failing session. You want four numbers: hardwareCost, systemPressureCost, and the fellUp/format summary for each device. If fellUp=true on the secondary, or systemPressureCost is near or above 1.0, this is your answer.
H3You cannot see the moment the connection diesdiagnostic gap

This is not a cause, but it is why rounds 1 and 2 could not converge. AVCaptureConnection.isActive is the system's verdict on whether media flows — distinct from isEnabled, which is only your own toggle and is what wireOutput logs at wire time. You read isActive once, in the census immediately after startRunning().

That read is too early. isActive settles asynchronously as the session spins up, and it is KVO-observable precisely because it changes over time. A connection that comes up active and is then deactivated under pressure is invisible in your current log — it looks identical to one that never activated.

FixAdd KVO observers on isActive for both video connections and log every transition with a timestamp. This converts "secondary absent for 3,399 frames" into "secondary went inactive at t=340 ms", which separates H1/H2 (deactivated under pressure) from a stream that never starts at all. Do this before the next field session — it is the highest-value change you can make.
H4Port selection uses an undocumented shortcutworth fixing regardless

Both wireOutput and ensurePipConnection select the input port with input.ports.first(where: { $0.mediaType == mediaType }). Apple's AVMultiCamPiP sample uses the explicit form instead:

input.ports(for: .video,
            sourceDeviceType: device.deviceType,
            sourceDevicePosition: device.position).first

The distinction is dormant while an input exposes exactly one video port and becomes real when it exposes several — which is precisely the kind of thing that changes when a sensor generation changes. Your sourceDeviceType: count across the whole module is zero, so this API is unused today.

I rank this below H1 and H2 because it does not explain why preview works and the data output does not — both paths use the same shortcut. But it is the documented API, it is a one-line change per site, and it removes a variable.

FixSwitch both call sites to ports(for:sourceDeviceType:sourceDevicePosition:) and log the resolved port count. If any device reports more than one video port, this moves straight to the top of the list.

03 — ProtocolThe next field session, one variable at a time

Rounds 1 and 2 each changed several things and produced an unchanged signature, which is what makes them hard to learn from. This sequence changes one thing per session and every step has a defined stopping condition.

00

Clear the persisted flags — before changing any code

Delete the app from the device. Not a TestFlight update; an actual deletion, which is what clears the exhibit.debug UserDefaults suite. Reinstall, open Diagnostics, confirm photoMaxDimensionsPolicy reads true, and capture.

Stop if this fixes it. If stereo commits, you have your answer and no Swift changes are needed — but add the guard in step 4 so a stale flag can never silently disable a mitigation again.

01

Read the census you already log

From a failing session, pull these four lines out of the diagnostics log before touching anything:

Log lineWhat it answers
multiCam census: hardwareCost=… systemPressureCost=…Is the graph over budget? (H2)
format chosen: device=…UltraWide… fellUp=…Did the secondary fall up out of its 720p budget? (H2)
configureSession started: … secondaryVideo=enabled=…,active=…Was the connection ever active? (H3)
wire secondary-video OK: … port=…Which port was resolved? (H4)

These four numbers will likely decide between H1 and H2 without writing a line of Swift.

02

Add isActive KVO, then capture once

Observe isActive on both video connections; log every transition with a millisecond timestamp relative to startRunning(). One session tells you which of two very different worlds you are in:

  • Never active → the connection was rejected at graph level. Go to step 3.
  • Active, then inactive at t=N → resource pressure. H1/H2 confirmed; note what else happens at t=N (the session-calibration one-shot, first photo capture, PiP attach).
03

Bisect the graph

Strip to the minimum that could possibly work — two video data outputs, nothing else. No photo outputs, no PiP connection, no audio. If the secondary delivers, add one consumer back per session in this order, since it runs cheapest to most expensive:

  1. audio output
  2. PiP preview connection
  3. primary photo output
  4. secondary photo output

The consumer that kills it is the answer. Four sessions, maximum. If the secondary never delivers even in the two-output minimum, cost is not the story — go to H4 and re-examine the device pair with supportedMultiCamDeviceSets logged explicitly.

04

Fixes worth making whatever the outcome

  • Gate on systemPressureCost, not just hardwareCost. Refusing honestly beats silently losing a stream — that is your own stated principle, and right now half the budget is unenforced.
  • Surface debug-flag state in the failure path. Your photo-failure dump prints two flags; print all five, and consider having a non-default flag value show a visible banner in Diagnostics. A persisted A/B flag silently disabling a mitigation across two release cycles is the kind of thing that should be loud.
  • Use the documented port API at both call sites (H4).
  • Validate the pair against supportedMultiCamDeviceSets before configuring, so an unsupported combination degrades to single-cam honestly instead of throwing NSInvalidArgumentException from inside a beginConfiguration block.
  • Reconsider the secondary photo output entirely. It is the single most expensive item on the graph and it exists to produce stereo stills. Those could come from the secondary video stream at far lower cost. Dropping it may be the structural fix rather than the workaround.

04 — NoteOn the blind-Swift problem

Both triage rounds end with the same residual risk: "No compiler; all edits re-verified by eye." That is the underlying constraint here — you are debugging a platform-specific resource-allocation problem through field reports, with a one-build-per-round feedback loop and no way to typecheck. It is why two well-reasoned rounds produced an unchanged signature.

The highest-leverage fix is not any hypothesis above; it is shortening that loop. Even without a full Xcode setup, swiftc -parse or swift-format --lint against the module would catch syntax and delimiter errors before a build, and the KVO change in step 2 converts your field signal from one bit ("absent") into a timeline. Between them you would learn more in one session than the last two rounds produced together.

For the launch itself, this does not need to be solved first — it needs to be stated. The open repo's stereo suites are lab-verified against fixtures and that claim is true and reproducible. Saying plainly that multi-camera capture currently degrades on iPhone 17 / iOS 26, and that the analyzers are verified independently of it, costs you far less than having someone discover the gap themselves.
